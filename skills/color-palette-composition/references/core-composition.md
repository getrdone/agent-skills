# Core Composition

<!-- Agent: Codex | Model: GPT-5 | Thinking: not exposed | Date: 2026-09-13 | Split from the former monolithic field guide as the required, compact composition reference. -->

This is the required reference for substantive new or revised themes. It treats hue as secondary to visual weight. A named wheel harmony or a 60/30/10 split is not enough by itself.

## Roles and the visual-weight check

- **Field:** dominant visual environment; quiet enough to live or read inside.
- **Partner:** secondary mass that gives character, structure, or depth.
- **Spark:** small focal contrast that tells the eye where to go.
- **Neutral / ink / on-color:** material or text color selected for undertone and contrast, not extra personality.
- **Semantic state:** success, warning, error, information, or a data category; separate it from decorative accent.

Use this directional heuristic, not a literal formula:

```
visual weight ~= area x chroma x local contrast
```

Temperature and isolation modify the result. A warm high-chroma accent on a quiet cool field can outweigh its literal area. A spark that wins area, chroma, hard edge, contrast, and isolation at once will overwhelm the composition.

| If the result feels… | First correction |
|---|---|
| Bland | Strengthen one focal advantage: value, chroma, temperature opposition, scale, or isolation. Do not add a fourth competing hue. |
| Busy | Quiet the field/partner, remove a redundant spark, or give vivid color one job only. |
| Flat | Increase value separation, material/texture contrast, or a controlled tonal step before changing hue. |
| Harsh | Reduce accent chroma or area, add quiet space, or replace a direct complement with a complementary mute or split relation. |
| Unclear | Re-name field, partner, and spark. If two colors could exchange jobs, subordinate or remove one. |

## Allocation is a starting hypothesis

Choose allocation by intended hierarchy, then correct it with Itten and Munsell. Never choose a split only because it is familiar.

| Split | Strong first trial when | Field / partner / spark placement | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **70 / 20 / 10** | editorial, campaign, thumbnail, presentation, or a room that needs a clear focal story | quiet field; structural/material partner; one emphasis | Preferred attention-led trial; the 10 still needs one task. |
| **80 / 15 / 5** | information-dense UI/report, refined identity, gallery-like interior | neutral canvas; subtle grouping; rare action/signal | Preferred restrained trial; isolation must make the 5 visible. |
| **60 / 30 / 10** | balanced traditional room, poster, or expressive brand | broad field; substantial partner; focal details | Documented interior convention, not default or law. |
| **90 / 8 / 2** | minimal identity, data-heavy interface, one rare signal | near-neutral field; faint structure; signal | The signal needs space and a meaningful job. |
| **80 / 20** | two-role Swiss/Apple-like layout, white plus wood, near-monochrome | field plus strong partner | Add only on-colors/states, not a decorative spark. |
| **60 / 30 / 5 / 5** | two subordinate details, such as brass plus one hue | field/partner plus two sparks | The two sparks cannot compete for one action. |
| **50 / 40 / 10** | campaign drama or two intentionally major forces | field plus near-equal partner; focal relief | Differentiate the 40 by value, texture, or material. |

### Two-second focal test

View the page, room elevation, or screen briefly. Can you identify the field, organizer, and focal spark in two seconds? Test in grayscale too: hue should reinforce structure, not be the only structure.

## Itten: choose the contrast that does the work

Choose at least one Itten contrast. If two chromatic areas coexist, explicitly decide whether contrast of extension should balance them or make one dominate.

| Itten contrast | What it controls | Useful decision |
|---|---|---|
| Hue | differentiated hue families | Make one family lead; do not give three pure hues equal authority. |
| Light-dark | value hierarchy | Set readable ink/on-colors and a grayscale focal path before relying on hue. |
| Cold-warm | advance/recede and environmental tension | Use a warm spark against a cool field, or invert deliberately; temperature is relative. |
| Complementary | strong opposition | Put one side in field/partner and restrain the other as spark or mute. |
| Simultaneous | neighbor-induced shifts | Test final materials, imagery, trim, or fills together; isolated chips lie. |
| Saturation | pure versus muted presence | Let spark be more chromatic, or mute one side of a fight. |
| Extension | unequal area for unequal light/power | Give less area to the lighter/louder hue unless dominance is intentional. |

**Itten/Goethe extension bias.** When two chromatic areas should feel comparably present, historical teaching uses approximate area ratios: yellow:violet = 1:3, orange:blue = 1:2, red:green = 1:1. Use less of the brighter/louder hue, or invert the bias deliberately for a signal. They are teaching approximations, not a paint formula or laboratory law.

## Munsell: select an HVC path and correct it

Munsell describes **Hue, Value, and Chroma (HVC)**:

| Axis | Means | Do not confuse it with |
|---|---|---|
| Hue | color family | personality or emotion |
| Value | light versus dark | automatically more or less color |
| Chroma | strength away from central gray at a given value | HSB saturation, a different measurement |

Name the path that builds the palette:

1. **Vertical path:** lighter and darker values of one hue—for coherent family and strong value hierarchy.
2. **Lateral path:** neighboring hues—for related/natural/restorative work; choose a hue leader.
3. **Inward through gray to the opposite hue:** reduced chroma toward an opposite family—for controlled complementary tension.

Then apply the Munsell **value-chroma correction**: a small intense color can balance a large muted field. Compare the relative Value × Chroma strength of competing chromatic areas and adjust area, chroma, or both. Treat inverse area to V×C as a balance check, not a calculation requirement.

## Named corrections

| Correction | Apply when | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Itten extension correction** | Two chromatic colors should feel equally present, or one should clearly dominate | Bias area away from the lighter/louder hue; use the historical teaching ratios as a starting experiment. |
| **Munsell value-chroma correction** | A high-chroma spark outweighs a muted field/partner | Reduce spark area/chroma, or strengthen field/partner value/chroma presence. |
| **Simultaneous-neighbor correction** | Samples, trim, textiles, image overlays, or adjacent fills alter each other | Test final neighbors together; align large-surface undertones or make tension deliberate. |
| **Albers quantity correction** | A named harmony looks wrong in placement, or dissonance is desired | Change quantity, scale, and placement before adding colors. Any chord can work when quantities are controlled. |
| **Cold-warm correction** | The composition lacks depth or spark fails to advance | Cross temperature at spark scale; keep broad surfaces in a compatible undertone family. |

## Harmony and family-building

Named wheel relationships are starting chords, not proof of beauty:

| Relationship | Best at | Make it work |
|---|---|---|
| Monochromatic | calm rooms, premium identities, compact spaces | Munsell vertical path, material contrast, perhaps one foreign spark. |
| Analogous | natural, cohesive, restorative work | Munsell lateral path; hue leader plus muted/reduced neighbors. |
| Complementary | sport, retail, bold focal energy | field/partner on one side; controlled spark/mute on the other; run extension and V×C checks. |
| Split-complementary | contrast with less aggression | One true spark; other split pale, structural, or absent. |
| Triadic | playful editorial, learning, expressive illustration | Unequal roles; never three equal saturated blocks. |
| Square/tetradic | fashion stories, complex maximalism | Quiet two hues into tones/neutrals and permit one spark. |
| Neutral plus signal | information design, timeless interiors, restrained brands | Let neutral dominate; spend signal only where attention matters. |

Build a related family before importing a second hue: field tint/tone; partner midtone; controlled chromatic midtone; structural shade; related near-white or near-black on-color.

## Sources and evidence boundary

- Johannes Itten, *The Art of Color*: seven contrasts, historical extension teaching, and dilution methods. His RYB wheel is a practical painterly model, not literal paint-mixing prediction.
- Albert H. Munsell, *A Color Notation*, and T. M. Cleland, *A Grammar of Color*: HVC organization and value/chroma balance as guidance, not quota.
- Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color*: contextual behavior, quantity, and placement.
