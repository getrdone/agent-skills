## Full mixing table

| Operation | Formula / HVC interpretation | Result | Best use | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hue | color family / change H | identity and naming | choose the leader family | Pure hue is rarely the right large field. |
| Tint | hue + white / raise Value, usually reduce relative chroma | lighter, airier family member | walls, canvas, quiet containers | Can look chalky or lose text contrast. |
| Tone | hue + gray / move inward in Chroma while controlling Value | quieter, mature family member | textiles, cards, secondary structures | Grays still carry undertones. |
| Shade | hue + black / lower Value, often reduce usable Chroma | deeper, heavier family member | cabinetry, night surfaces, structural depth | Can deaden/muddy a color; test real material. |
| Complementary mute | hue + opposite / inward through gray toward opposite | controlled earth or mature neutral | restrained partner, transition, soft contrast | Too much collapses into mud. |
| Near-white on-color | related high-Value neutral | legible light foreground | text, trim, marks on dark fills | Verify actual contrast. |
| Near-black on-color | related low-Value neutral | legible dark foreground | body text, framing, marks on light fills | Avoid undertone that fights the field. |

### Mixing system matters

| Medium | Working system | Mix direction | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen | additive RGB, then HCT/OKLCH or system tokens | light adds toward white | A screen complement is not automatically a paint complement. |
| Pigment/interior | practical RYB language and physical samples | mixed pigment tends duller/browner | Itten's diagram does not reliably predict literal paint mixing. |
| Process print | CMYK plus intended ICC profile, stock, and press | ink/paper constrain result | Generic RGB→CMYK values are not production approval. |

## Undertones and temperature

Every neutral leans. White can be cream, pink, or icy; gray can be blue, green, violet, or brown. Keep large continuous surfaces in compatible temperature/undertone families. Cross warm/cool at spark scale, or bridge the tension with repeated wood, stone, textile, or metal.

Blue-white trim beside a yellow-cream wall can make both look dirty. Test samples beside all fixed materials under actual light. Same-color wall/trim can be an intentional wrap rather than a compromise.

## Build and place the theme

1. Record fixed constraints: official colors, existing floor/stone/wood, imagery, stock, lighting, audience, and accessibility needs.
2. State concept in 3–6 words and name one attention target.
3. Choose field plus readable ink/on-color; prefer tint/tone over a pure full-chroma field unless immersion is the brief.
4. Select allocation and apply the Itten extension and Munsell value-chroma corrections from core composition.
5. Build the related family with the mixing table; only then select a wheel relationship if it helps.
6. Assign field, partner, spark, neutral/on-color, semantic roles, canonical story names, and semantic aliases.
7. Map each role to an actual surface, material, component, or focal detail. A color with no placement has no job.
8. Write theory summary and run focal, contrast, grayscale, color-vision, and output/light tests.

## Role map by medium

| Role | Interior | Graphic/editorial | UI/product/data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field | walls, ceiling/floor, large upholstery, continuous wood/stone | canvas, whitespace, photo field, paper | canvas, background, low-emphasis surface |
| Partner | rug, drapery, cabinetry, furniture family, major plane | type family, sideband, illustration mass, section panel | navigation, grouping surface, chart context, secondary container |
| Spark | art, lamp, cushion, door, flowers, small hardware | CTA, key number, pull quote, logo mark, motif | primary action, selected state, focus, key datum; never all controls |
| Ink/on-color | trim, dark metal, framing contrast | body copy and required labels | `on-surface`, `on-primary`, icon/label colors |
| Semantic | wayfinding or alert with words/signs | explanatory icon, label, or pattern | success/warning/error/info with cue beyond hue |

### Interiors

Inventory floor, countertop, tile, stone, fireplace, cabinet finish, upholstery, and metals before choosing paint. Put field on continuous planes; give partner genuine mass through rug, drapery, cabinetry, or furniture; repeat spark two or three times in a sightline. Test samples in installed morning, midday, evening, and night lighting. Consider LRV, sheen, room orientation, and whole-home transitions.

### Graphic, editorial, and campaign

Begin with 70/20/10 when a clear story or thumbnail is the goal: 70 for image/page field, 20 for type/image-grade/section structure, 10 for one focal cue. Keep reading text in ink/on-color; test at phone size. A composition needs only one loudest object.

### UI and product

Begin with 80/15/5 for information-dense work. Define `canvas`, `surface`, `surface-raised`, `ink`, `ink-muted`, `border`, `action`, `focus`, and semantic states. Pair every fill with explicit `on-*` foreground. Do not color every card, button, or status.

### Data visualization

Do not force categorical data into field/partner/spark ratios. Use color for meaningful category, sequential range, or diverging midpoint; pair it with direct labels, patterns, line styles, markers, ordering, or annotation. Spend spark on the takeaway, not every series.

### Print

Lock canonical RGB/HEX master, retain intended ICC profile, obtain printer/stock conditions, soft-proof, and approve proof when color matters. Make hierarchy work in grayscale. A profile improves consistency; it cannot make a screen HEX equal to ink or paint.
