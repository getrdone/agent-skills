# The Framework — full method

Source: The Futur workshop, Chris Do × Aprilynne Alter (YouTube strategist). Core thesis: **the idea sets the ceiling; the execution determines the result.** Everything below happens *before* recording.

## Stage 1 — Ideation

**Generate (quantity first).** Idea generation is a daily habit, not a weekly scramble (top creators time-box ~10 min/day). An idea = **a unique combination of two or more familiar things**, built as **Topic × Format × Angle**:
- **Topic** — what the video is about.
- **Format** — how value is delivered: tutorial (how-to), challenge, commentary/reaction, breakdown/video-essay, vlog, podcast/interview.
- **Angle** — the spin: beginner, expert, money, versus/comparison, time-bound, etc.

Combining these turns one topic into 10–50 ideas.

**Filter (quality).** Score each idea on two axes:
- **TAM (reach / ceiling).** How many people *can* this reach? Validate with **proof of prior success**: search the topic and look for **outliers** — videos that beat *their own channel's* average (not just high absolute views, which may reflect the creator, not the topic). Weigh outlier view levels against **the user's own view goal**: 15–20k outliers are great proof if the goal is 10k, risky if the goal is 100k.
- **Remarkability (novelty).** Is it novel enough to be *remarked upon*? Absence of prior proof can itself be the novelty — higher risk, and you must create your own proof.

Gate: greenlight only when both axes clear the user's bar. Express the idea as a rough working title.

## Stage 2 — Title (packaging #1)

**Packaging ≠ idea.** A title is not "what the video is about" — it's **the presentation that best earns the click and still satisfies the viewer afterward** (the "broccoli as a gift" framing: sell the benefit, then deliver the substance).

**Generate 30+ titles in ~30 min:**
1. 5 min — brainstorm from your head.
2. 5–10 min — extract *format patterns* from your 5 **model channels** (same format, different niche): "how to ___ without ___".
3. 5–10 min — scan **outlier tools** (e.g. 1of10) for recurring power words/patterns (e.g. time frames — "in 30 days"); consider reshaping the idea to fit a strong recurring pattern.
4. last 5 min — paste your list into an expander with what you like/dislike and ask for 50 more (seeding with your list keeps it on-pattern and off colon-spam).

**Title rules:**
- Skimmable and short — aim under ~65 characters.
- Most important / curiosity-inducing words in the **upper-left** (we read left→right, top→bottom).
- ~5th-grade reading level; prefer shorter synonyms.
- Exactly **one strong curiosity gap** (gap between what the viewer knows and wants to know).
- Use complex/technical wording only when the audience is **solution-aware** and searching that term.

**Search vs browse:**
- **Browse** — problem-aware viewers, highest ceiling, most of YouTube's views. Default.
- **Search** — solution-aware viewers, easier to win when starting out or for specific/technical problems. Use keyword tools (vidIQ, TubeBuddy) for high-volume / low-competition terms; add keywords to title/description/spoken audio.

You can AB test up to 3–5 titles.

## Stage 3 — Click triggers

Every click is one of three emotions (term credited to Jake Thomas). Same topic, different framing → different audience and audience size:
- **Desire** — chasing an outcome. "Boost your testosterone in 20 days."
- **Fear** — avoiding a loss. "Why you're losing muscle mass."
- **Curiosity** — wanting to know an outcome. "Brother vs sister testosterone challenge."

Broad triggers (news/novelty) maximize TAM; narrow triggers (a specific fear/desire) qualify a smaller, higher-intent audience. Match the trigger to whether the goal is reach or conversion.

## Stage 4 — Thumbnail (packaging #2)

Thumbnail works *with* the title to open the curiosity gap.
1. **List content elements** — what visuals/text represent each part of the title (20 days → calendar; $100M → cash / green text; views → play button / analytics arrows).
2. **Pick a format matching the curiosity gap:**
   - **Result** — show the outcome ("super jacked, in 20 days").
   - **Before/after transformation** — show start and end states.
   - **Novelty** — show something never seen before (pairs with curiosity triggers).
   - **Comparison / tier-list** — left vs right, or ranked options.
3. **Tease, never reveal.** The viewer must not be able to guess the answer from the thumbnail — hint at "what does work," don't hand over the payoff.

## Stage 5 — Story & retention

Clicks get them in; **payoffs** keep them watching.
- **Every video needs a payoff**, not necessarily a moral. Pick the payoff type the audience wants — emotional vs informational (a Procreate tutorial doesn't need an emotional arc).
- **Emotion aids memory** — even educators retain more viewers by evoking emotion; no sob story required.
- **Promise → Progress → Payoff.** State the goal up front, deliver escalating mini-payoffs, resolve at the end. **Start at the crash** — open at the highest-friction moment, cut the setup.
- **The subject is the protagonist, not the creator.** In case studies you're the mentor (Gandalf to their Frodo); the emotion can be *their* emotion. Tell it partly in third person, partly in "she had this problem, so we did this."
- **Format = 4 ingredients** (makes it repeatable):
  - **Constant** — never changes (the hook / recurring structure).
  - **Variable** — what changes (usually the person/case).
  - **Tension** — delay the conclusion; create stakes.
  - **Payoff** — "did they or didn't they?"
  Formulaic is a feature: Kitchen Nightmares, rom-coms, and Robert Green's *48 Laws of Power* all reuse one skeleton. Format-less "art films" have no audience.
- **Land the plane** — end with why you told the story so viewers connect the dots.
- **Idea creators vs relationship creators** — idea creators live on per-video novelty; relationship creators build audiences who return for *them*, with more long-term leverage. Evoking emotion / character moves a channel toward the relationship end.

## Cross-cutting principles

- **You're in the jar** — you can't read your own label; use external signal to judge ideas.
- **Idea vs packaging** — diagnose which one drove a hit before trying to replicate it; isolate one variable at a time.
- **Audience states** — problem-aware vs solution-aware determines search/browse and simple/technical language.
