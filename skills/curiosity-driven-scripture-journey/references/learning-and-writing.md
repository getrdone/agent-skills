# Learning and Writing Architecture

Use this file to plan topics, teaching flow, progressive disclosure, interaction, or explanatory writing across channels.

## Learning map

Define these before outlining substantial content:

1. **Known anchor:** a familiar fact, verse, experience, image, or question that activates prior knowledge.
2. **New idea:** the specific unfamiliar answer or distinction the learner should understand.
3. **Scaffolding:** the smallest ordered steps that bridge known to new without a leap.
4. **Evidence moments:** the text, document, example, comparison, or visual that makes each step concrete.
5. **Chunks:** meaningful groups small enough for working memory; one dominant purpose per chunk.
6. **Signals:** headings, emphasis, narration, maps, or visual cues that show what matters and where the learner is.
7. **Participation:** a low-pressure comparison, prediction, tap, reveal, reflection, or retrieval prompt that helps processing without grading the learner.
8. **Payoff ladder:** small answers throughout, followed by the promised primary answer.
9. **Reflection/transfer:** a plain prompt that connects the insight to another text, question, or real-life meaning without coercing a conclusion.

## Learning principles

- **Known to unknown:** open with a useful familiar anchor, then move toward the new idea.
- **Cognitive load:** remove decorative or verbal noise; chunk inherent complexity; reserve attention for understanding.
- **Progressive disclosure:** reveal detail as it becomes useful. Never hide core content behind interaction or delay a promised answer merely to keep attention.
- **Signaling:** use hierarchy and previews to make the path visible.
- **Dual coding:** pair words with purposeful visuals, diagrams, timelines, comparisons, or audio when each channel clarifies the same truth. Do not assign people fixed “learning styles.”
- **Scaffolding/ZPD:** keep each step slightly beyond the previous one, with enough context to cross the gap.
- **Adult learning:** explain why the question matters, respect existing experience, preserve agency, and favor problem-centered exploration.
- **Retrieval without threat:** optional recall or prediction may strengthen memory, but interactions must not embarrass, grade, or trick beginners.
- **Minimal repetition:** repeat only when it reorients, compresses, or deepens the idea.

## Writing strategy router

Curiosity is constant; rhetorical structure varies. Select a strategy because it fits the reader and content, not to create a house style.

- **Curiosity core:** sincere question → available evidence → accumulating clarity → answer → next question. Default for discovery content.
- **AIDA:** attention → interest → desire → action. Use for a clear offer or invitation; keep action voluntary.
- **PAS:** problem → consequence → solution. Use only when the problem is real and consequence language does not amplify fear.
- **BAB:** before → after → bridge. Useful for a change in understanding or practical experience.
- **4Ps:** promise → picture → proof → proposal. Useful when evidence and a concrete next step are central.
- **FAB:** feature → advantage → benefit. Useful for tools, formats, courses, or practical offers.
- **4Cs:** clear → concise → compelling → credible. Use mainly as an editing pass.

Combine at most what the piece needs. Record the chosen strategy in the design/content fingerprint to reduce repetition across projects.

## Language and voice

- Address one intelligent adult reader directly or use neutral phrasing.
- In Scripture studies, avoid a teacher persona, “we,” “friend,” adversarial proof language, and language that assumes the conclusion.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs, short paragraphs, and one idea per sentence where possible.
- Define necessary terms immediately: “justification—being declared right with God.”
- Separate facts/observation, interpretation, application, and speculation when blending them would overstate certainty.

## NLP-labeled techniques

Use only as conservative clarity aids:

- multimodal presentation of the same true content;
- genuine audience-aware tone and pacing;
- clarification of vague language;
- modeling effective, honest teaching structure;
- chunking up to the big picture and down to concrete detail.

Exclude hypnotic/vague language, covert influence, emotional conditioning, spin, manipulation, and the entire “seduction” branch. If uncertain, use plain, explicit communication.
