# Learning Pattern Evidence and Platform Flows

Use this file to audit, extend, or explain the learning-pattern catalog. It records what leading platforms actually place in the learner’s path and how this project adapts those mechanics. It is not a license to copy proprietary content, interface artwork, wording, or reward systems.

Last reviewed: **2026-08-21**.

## Evidence labels

- **Observed flow:** visible on a public course, lesson, simulation, product guide, or first-party support page.
- **Platform-described behavior:** the provider explains how the product works; this is useful design evidence, not independent proof of effectiveness.
- **Project adaptation:** a Scripture-safe implementation derived from the observed behavior and this skill’s truth, dignity, accessibility, and source-integrity rules.
- **Research foundation:** an external learning or accessibility source supports the underlying principle. It does not prove that a particular platform implementation causes its business or learning outcomes.

## Detailed platform notes

### Khan Academy

**Observed flow**

1. A course page exposes units, skills, mastery points, quizzes, unit tests, and a course challenge.
2. Units can combine videos, articles, practice exercises, quizzes, and a unit test.
3. Practice offers graduated solution steps; assessments delay help until after an attempt in some contexts.
4. Mastery levels make progress visible from Not Started through Mastered.
5. Mastery Challenges later revisit three previously learned skills with mixed questions.

**What to transfer**

- prerequisite-aware sequencing;
- worked reasoning followed by practice;
- help on demand;
- delayed mixed retrieval;
- explicit but calm progress state.

**What not to transfer literally**

- do not mark a Scripture observation “wrong” because help was opened;
- do not turn mastery points into spiritual status;
- do not make a long course tree the only way to reach a sincere question.

**First-party references**

- [Example unit: Multiply and divide multi-digit numbers](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/Arithmetic/multiplication-division)
- [What are Mastery Challenges?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037494231-What-are-Mastery-Challenges)
- [How mastery levels work](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work)
- [Accessibility settings](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360015623271)

### Khan Academy Kids

**Observed flow**

1. A child profile supplies an age-appropriate starting point.
2. The play path serves activities, books, videos, and creative lessons and adapts with performance.
3. The Library provides an independent, self-selected route.
4. Books can be read aloud or read independently; reading experiences may highlight words and ask supportive comprehension questions.
5. Friendly characters model, prompt, comment, and encourage.

**What to transfer**

- guided route plus learner-controlled library;
- short, varied but equivalent activities;
- narration and follow-along text;
- calm characters or guide voice only when it fits the project personality;
- adaptation based on the current task, not a fixed label.

**What not to transfer literally**

- do not make adult novice content childlike;
- do not use mascot praise as a substitute for explanatory feedback.

**First-party references**

- [Khan Academy Kids overview](https://learn.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-kids/)
- [Early reading and ELA experience](https://www.khanacademy.org/kids/ela)
- [Getting started and the personalized path](https://www.khanacademy.org/khan-for-educators/resources/parents-mentors-1/khan-academy-accounts/v/getting-started-with-khan-academy-kids)

### Brilliant

**Observed flow**

1. Learning Paths order courses from foundations to advanced application.
2. Lessons mix clear explanations with hands-on problems rather than separating all exposition from all practice.
3. Visual simulations and animations make abstract relationships manipulable.
4. The learner receives instant feedback; current product descriptions say custom feedback catches mistakes while the learner works.
5. Practice checkpoints, progress tracking, and personalized sets revisit gaps.
6. The Koji tutor asks questions and guides thinking instead of simply supplying an answer.

**What to transfer**

- interleave explanation and action;
- use visuals as reasoning tools;
- make feedback specific to the learner’s action;
- guide with questions and partial steps;
- move from foundations to applied problems.

**What not to transfer literally**

- do not gate essential Scripture content behind subscription-like keys or sequential locks;
- do not use streaks, leagues, or XP as the main reason to continue;
- do not claim Brilliant’s marketing effectiveness numbers for this project.

**First-party references**

- [Courses and curriculum](https://brilliant.org/help/courses-and-curriculum/)
- [Learning Paths](https://brilliant.org/help/features/what-are-learning-paths/)
- [Brilliant basics and learner flow](https://brilliant.org/help/using-brilliant/)
- [Product overview](https://brilliant.org/)

### Duolingo

**Observed flow**

1. The home path gives one next step and groups lessons into smaller units.
2. Lessons mix concepts instead of exhausting one skill before touching another.
3. Stories and unit guidebooks are placed in the same path as regular lessons.
4. Interactive exercises require an early response and draw attention to patterns.
5. Explain My Answer can give immediate, error-specific grammar or vocabulary guidance.
6. Mistake review and focused practice revisit weak skills; spaced repetition returns concepts after increasing delays.
7. Progress scores, streaks, quests, and characters make momentum visible.

**What to transfer**

- one obvious next action;
- short sessions with meaningful completion;
- mixed and spaced return;
- immediate explanation tied to the response;
- visible progress based on learning milestones.

**What not to transfer literally**

- no streak anxiety, hearts, leaderboards, scarcity, or loss aversion;
- no cheerful animation in place of interpretive nuance;
- no daily-use pressure for sensitive spiritual content.

**First-party references**

- [The Duolingo teaching method](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-teaching-method/)
- [Science behind the linear learning path](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)
- [Explain My Answer](https://blog.duolingo.com/explain-my-answer-now-free/)
- [Practice tab and mistake review](https://blog.duolingo.com/guide-to-duolingo-practice-hub/)

### BBC Bitesize

**Observed flow**

1. Learners narrow the catalog by nation, level, subject, exam board, or specification.
2. Revision guides recap key ideas with short explanations, bullets, and labeled diagrams.
3. Many guides add video or audio for another explanation route.
4. Multiple-choice quizzes follow most guides; exam-practice questions add transfer.
5. Learners can save guides and personalize the subject/specification view.

**What to transfer**

- scope the material to a clear audience and curriculum need;
- compress without removing the reasoning;
- add media only when it clarifies;
- place a quick check or application after the explanation;
- let the learner save or resume.

**What not to transfer literally**

- a Scripture Journey is not exam revision; remove test pressure and specification jargon;
- concise does not mean reducing contested interpretation to a fact card.

**First-party reference**

- [BBC Bitesize app listing by BBC Media Applications Technologies Limited](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/bbc-bitesize-exam-revision/id1089215244)

### PhET Interactive Simulations

**Observed flow**

1. A simulation opens with a scene and a small set of screens or experiment modes.
2. Controls let learners adjust meaningful variables, pause, measure, compare, graph, and reset.
3. Dynamic feedback makes invisible relationships visible immediately.
4. Teacher guidance recommends curiosity, productive struggle, prediction, testing, observation, and explanation.
5. Inclusive simulations add alternative input, enhanced touch, highlights, pan/zoom, sound or sonification, interactive descriptions, and in-sim language selection.
6. Accessible state descriptions update as the model changes.

**Concrete example**

The Energy Skate Park simulation moves from an Intro screen to Measure, Graphs, and Playground: explore energy, measure and compare it, analyze it by position or time, then build a custom track.

**What to transfer**

- direct manipulation with an immediate consequence;
- predict-observe-explain cycles;
- progressively more powerful representations;
- reset and free exploration after guided orientation;
- equivalent dynamic descriptions.

**What not to transfer literally**

- do not turn interpretive claims into a physics-like model that implies deterministic certainty;
- do not use motion or sound without meaningful text state;
- do not let free exploration obscure the promised answer.

**First-party references**

- [Teaching with PhET](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/teaching-resources)
- [Energy Skate Park interactive flow](https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/energy-skate-park/latest/energy-skate-park_en.html)
- [Inclusive features](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/inclusive-design/features)
- [Accessible dynamic-state example](https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/resistance-in-a-wire/1.6.10/resistance-in-a-wire_a11y_view.html)

### CK-12

**Observed flow**

1. A FlexBook lesson places core text, PLIX interactives, simulations, adaptive practice, and curated related modalities in one lesson environment.
2. Learners can highlight text and change font size, color, and line spacing.
3. “View Practice” opens practice beside the lesson.
4. Practice is untimed, allows multiple attempts, and includes hints, scratchpad, immediate feedback, helpful resources, and recommendations.
5. Difficulty rises or falls with performance; struggling learners receive relevant reading or video suggestions.
6. Quizzes remove those supports and return feedback at the end, making a clear practice-versus-assessment distinction.

**What to transfer**

- keep explanation, interaction, and support close together;
- offer equivalent modalities and display settings;
- distinguish supported practice from later self-check;
- adapt the next problem or resource to the observed gap;
- keep concept maps available for relationship-heavy topics.

**What not to transfer literally**

- a public Scripture Journey should not require an LMS or teacher report;
- do not collect unnecessary learner data for personalization;
- do not copy or remix CK-12 content without complying with its curriculum license.

**First-party references**

- [FlexBook lesson features](https://help.ck12.org/hc/en-us/articles/360008241774-FlexBook-2-0-Lesson-Features)
- [Adaptive Practice](https://int-www.ck12.org/practice)
- [Practice versus quizzes](https://help.ck12.org/hc/en-us/articles/203532454-Practice-vs-Quizzes)
- [Concept maps](https://help.ck12.org/hc/en-us/articles/200344405-What-Is-a-Concept-Map)

### Coursera

**Observed flow**

1. Before enrollment, a course page states outcomes, skills, level, prerequisite expectation, flexible schedule, total or weekly workload, module count, language, and assessment count.
2. Module previews expose the mix and estimated time for videos, readings, assignments, discussions, and peer reviews.
3. Orientation gives the learner the course map and technical expectations.
4. Modules move through explanation and checks toward hands-on projects, peer work, or a capstone that integrates earlier concepts.

**What to transfer**

- give adults an honest contract before they invest attention;
- expose workload and route;
- mix media and activities inside a purposeful module;
- end with application or synthesis rather than another summary;
- support flexible pacing.

**What not to transfer literally**

- do not make a Scripture Journey feel like a credential program;
- do not use peer review when privacy, doctrine, or moderation would make it unsafe;
- do not make long workload estimates look like proof of depth.

**First-party references**

- [Course landing-page contract example](https://www.coursera.org/learn/initiating-a-research-project)
- [Module mix and timing example](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ux-design-concept-wireframe)
- [Concept-to-capstone sequence example](https://www.coursera.org/learn/innovation-creativity-entrepreneurship-capstone)

### CodeAI, formerly Code.org

**Observed flow**

1. A lesson plan states time, overview, goal, standards, agenda, and question of the day.
2. Lessons can begin with a warm-up or short video, then move through numbered level bubbles.
3. Level sequences combine skill building, practice, checks for understanding, a build or free-play project, challenge, and reflection.
4. Programming levels provide Run/Test behavior; validation returns test results and enables completion after requirements pass.
5. Teachers can return work to “Keep Working,” add feedback, and review project rubrics.
6. The instruction panel includes Immersive Reader and text-to-speech.
7. The organization became CodeAI in June 2026; existing Code.org courses and materials remain supported.

**What to transfer**

- short skill-building steps that culminate in creation;
- explicit run/test/observe loops;
- formative checkpoints and revision;
- free exploration after enough scaffolding;
- reflection after making;
- built-in reading assistance.

**What not to transfer literally**

- a green completion bubble is not evidence that a theological conclusion is true;
- do not automatically validate open interpretation as if it were code;
- do not require an account or teacher oversight for the core public journey.

**First-party references**

## Research and accessibility foundations

Use these sources for underlying principles, not as claims that every implementation automatically works.

- **Retrieval practice:** Roediger and Karpicke found that retrieving studied prose improved delayed retention compared with repeated study. [Test-Enhanced Learning](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)
- **Distributed practice:** Cepeda and colleagues reviewed spacing effects across verbal-learning studies. [Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)
- **Worked examples:** Sweller and Cooper compared worked examples with conventional problem solving during skill acquisition. [The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving](https://doi.org/10.1016/0364-0213(85)90023-7)
- **Active learning:** Freeman and colleagues’ meta-analysis found improved STEM performance under active learning compared with traditional lecturing. [Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111)
- **Learning-style caution:** Pashler and colleagues found no adequate evidence for assigning instruction by fixed learning-style classifications, while recognizing that learners have preferences and differing aptitudes. [Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x)
- **Accessible interaction and media:** use [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and the [W3C media accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/) for keyboard access, alternatives, captions, transcripts, focus, contrast, and dynamic status.

## Repository maintenance rule

When adding a platform or changing a pattern:

1. cite a current first-party course, lesson, product guide, help page, or design/research page;
2. write the actual learner sequence, not only a marketing adjective;
3. separate observed behavior from the Scripture adaptation;
4. name what must not be copied or transferred;
5. update the relevant pattern card and fast selector;
6. preserve the no-fixed-learning-style, no-belief-scoring, core-content-visible, and accessible-equivalence constraints;
7. update the review date.
