# Story Craft and Claim Discipline

**Agent: Buffy · Model: GPT-5 · Thinking: not exposed · Date: 2026-09-24**

Use this reference for video scripts, story-led teaching, illustrations, scene design, and narrative transfer. It supplements `learning-and-writing.md`; it does not replace Scripture authority, source governance, or the shared high-trust rules.

## Core rule

A story is a **meaning-carrying scene**. It earns its place when it helps a learner see, remember, compare, question, or transfer a true idea. It must not replace evidence, delay the promised answer, invent history, or manufacture emotion.

For Scripture:

- Scripture and its context remain the authority.
- An illustration may scaffold interpretation but cannot establish doctrine.
- Facts, interpretation, application, and uncertainty must remain distinguishable.
- A creator's personal testimony may be labeled as testimony; it is not automatically general proof.

## Optional story architecture

Use this as a diagnostic for a human or source-backed story. It is not a mandatory formula and not a reason to add a story to every piece.

1. **Hook:** name an honest question, tension, contrast, or recognizable problem. Confirm the package and begin useful context.
2. **Stakes:** show the real obstacle, uncertainty, consequence, or reason the question matters. Never invent suffering to create urgency.
3. **Turn:** introduce a verified discovery, new evidence, changed comparison, or honest complication.
4. **Scene:** make the moment concrete with details that serve comprehension.
5. **Meaning:** state what the scene helps the learner understand and why it matters now.
6. **Answer / payoff:** deliver what the evidence supports; state meaningful limits or uncertainty.
7. **Next curiosity:** offer a truthful continuation, reflection, comparison, or application.

The fifth step is mandatory when a story or illustration is used: **a scene without a meaning handoff is decoration**.

## Meaning handoff

After the story, answer these questions in plain language:

- What did the learner notice?
- What does the example explain?
- What does the example not prove?
- What is observation, and what is interpretation?
- What remains uncertain?
- How can the learner apply, test, or transfer the insight without pressure?

For a Bible study, the handoff must point back to the passage, context, and source trail. “The reason I am telling you this is because…” is a useful drafting prompt, but the answer must be specific and evidenced.

## PAST scene card

Use PAST when a scene needs to be more concrete:

- **Place:** where is the event? State only what the source supports.
- **Action:** what is visibly happening? Prefer concrete verbs over setup.
- **Speech:** what words were actually said? Quote only verified words. Label an approximate reconstruction as a reconstruction.
- **Thoughts:** what inner state is actually known? Do not invent a character's thoughts.

For contemporary personal stories, sensory detail may be used carefully. For Scripture, PAST cannot fill historical, verbal, or psychological gaps. The text itself is the primary evidence.

## Delivery pass

Before recording or publishing, check:

- **Conversational test:** would this line be reasonable to say to one respectful person? If not, revise it without losing clarity.
- **One-person address:** speak to one learner even when the audience is large.
- **Meaningful pause:** mark a pause only where it helps the listener absorb a turn, distinction, or evidence—not to manufacture drama.
- **Pronunciation:** say names, terms, and references clearly.
- **Emotional trajectory:** decide what the learner should feel, understand, and optionally do. Emotion serves meaning; it is not a demand.
- **Analogy or visual bridge:** use a familiar comparison when it makes a new idea easier to inspect. Check what the analogy does not explain.
- **Simplicity:** use a child-comprehension test for difficult ideas: can a new reader restate the idea in ordinary words? This is a clarity test, not a claim that children have a special fixed learning style or that all people think alike.
- **Length:** be long enough to cover the subject and short enough to respect attention. Do not treat a fixed duration as a law.

## Claim ledger and rejection rules

The following source claims are retained as **research notes and rejection checks**, not as accepted operating rules.

| Claim or idea | Status in this skill | Safe use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Stories are 21 times more powerful than facts.” | Unverified and rejected as a universal rule. | Use stories when they clarify a real question; do not publish the multiplier without a precise, independently checked source. |
| “Stories activate the left brain and right brain in this exact way.” | Oversimplified and rejected as a mechanism. | Explain content clearly; do not use hemisphere claims as proof. |
| “People have an eight-second attention span, lower than a goldfish.” | Unsupported as a universal rule. | Use honest curiosity and early value; do not prescribe one attention-span number. |
| “Mirror neurons make an audience feel exactly what the speaker feels.” | Overstated and rejected as an exact mechanism. | Authentic expression may support connection; do not promise identical audience feelings. |
| “A hook must work in three seconds.” | Useful only as a short-form diagnostic, not a law. | Confirm the promise early; give enough context for the channel and learner. |
| “Explain it simply so a child will understand it.” | Keep as a plain-language test with limits. | Use ordinary words and one clear analogy; never assign a fixed learning style or make claims about children's cognition. |
| “The subconscious is permanently a child.” | Set aside pending evidence; not an operating rule. | Do not use as psychology, neuroscience, or doctrine. |
| “Theta states or affirmations reliably reprogram the mind.” | Rejected as a reliable-method claim. | Do not prescribe hypnosis, theta states, or affirmations as Scripture formation tools. |
| “The universe must make an assumed outcome real.” | Absolutely false as stated; reject. | Use Scripture, evidence, prayer, and faithful action without manifestation guarantees. |
| “Following these steps will produce virality, sales, or a standing ovation.” | Not guaranteed; reject as a promise. | Measure outcomes honestly; never guarantee reach, sales, or reception. |
| Any Scripture doctrine based on personal testimony, sales results, or neuroscience-sounding claims. | Reject as authority. | Use Scripture compared with Scripture, then named historical sources under the authority rules. |

When a claim is uncertain, the correct behavior is not to repeat it more softly. Mark it **unverified**, narrow it, or remove it.

## Story selection and transfer

A story is usually helpful when:

- the question is meaningful or emotionally relevant;
- a concrete example makes an abstract idea easier to inspect;
- the learner needs a case, contrast, or safe mistake;
- a personal or historical narrative can be accurately sourced;
- the story clarifies the passage rather than competing with it.

A direct explanation may be better when:

- the answer is simple and urgent;
- the evidence is short and precise;
- the question is factual rather than narrative;
- a story would delay the answer or add unsupported claims.

For reflection, use **identity-safe transfer**:

- invite the learner to name one truthful practice, habit, or identity shift connected to the passage;
- connect it to the text and evidence;
- offer one small, voluntary step;
- do not promise rewiring, healing, guaranteed behavior change, or spiritual status;
- provide a private written or equivalent accessible response.

## Final craft gate

Before a story-led artifact is approved:

- The governing promise is clear.
- The hook is honest and gives value early.
- Stakes are real and proportionate.
- The turn is evidence-based.
- PAST details are sourced or labeled.
- The meaning handoff is explicit.
- The answer and uncertainty are stated plainly.
- The delivery pass is complete.
- Unsupported neuroscience, psychology, sales, and outcome claims are removed or marked unverified.
- Scripture is not replaced by anecdote, personal authority, or a guaranteed emotional result.
