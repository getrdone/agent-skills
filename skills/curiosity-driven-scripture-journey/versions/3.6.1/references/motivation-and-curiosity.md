## Motivation starts with the learner's question

Treat motivation as a changing reason to engage with a particular question. A person can seek understanding, enjoy discovery, fear misunderstanding, and prepare for a social responsibility at the same time. Do not infer a personality type from clicks, religious background, an anxious question, or one chosen branch.

During concept development, record a plausible **toward goal** and, where present, an **away concern**. Mark each as supplied by the user/audience research or as a hypothesis. A toward goal names something the learner values gaining, understanding, or doing. An away concern names a real misunderstanding, loss, uncertainty, or consequence the learner wants to avoid or resolve. Some pieces need only one direction; do not invent fear to fill a matrix.

### The four-part matrix as a prompt

The remembered internal-positive, external-positive, internal-negative, external-negative matrix is a practitioner framework (evidence M1). Its positive/negative axis is related to approach/avoidance, but the terms are not identical scientific measures. Use the following as questions during planning, not labels displayed on people:

| Matrix language | Possible reason to engage | Useful content response |
|---|---|---|
| Internal positive | Interest, enjoyment of discovery, satisfaction from understanding | Offer a worthwhile question, visible evidence, and a satisfying explanation. |
| External positive | Appreciation, recognition, or a concrete benefit associated with learning | Explain the practical benefit accurately; let the learning remain worthwhile without praise or a reward. |
| Internal negative | Worry about being wrong or feeling inadequate | Make uncertainty safe, explain prerequisites, and offer private practice with help. Never activate shame to gain attention. |
| External negative | Concern about social judgment or an actual adverse consequence | Acknowledge the concern proportionately, inspect the relevant evidence, and preserve choice. Never threaten exclusion or belonging. |

These are possible circumstances, not four permanent groups. They do not establish that loss is the strongest motive, that goal-oriented people are organized, or that a learner must fit one quadrant. The research on approach/avoidance framing and planning does not justify those claims (M2).

Keep a further distinction clear: **intrinsic/extrinsic motivation in self-determination theory is not equivalent to internal/external in this matrix**. An externally useful activity can be freely valued, and inward self-pressure can still feel controlling (M4). A learner choosing to understand Scripture for a meaningful purpose does not need to find every section entertaining.

Approach/avoidance framing can change the emotional experience of the same task (M5), even when other outcomes do not differ. Make valued understanding and growing capability the usual destination of the journey, and address an existing concern openly where relevant. Loss aversion concerns particular choices relative to a reference point (M6); it is not a universal explanation of why learners engage.

### A usable motivation brief

Add these compact fields to the existing Journey Concept or script plan:

- **Toward:** the valued understanding or ability the work can actually provide.
- **Away, if relevant:** the concern already present; its basis and proportional response.
- **Why now:** a real reason this question matters, without invented urgency.
- **Agency:** how the learner can inspect, question, pause, skip an exercise, or choose further depth.
- **Progress:** what the learner will be able to notice or explain after the next meaningful step.

Example for a comparison study: toward = understanding why two passages seem different; away = worry about quoting one out of context; agency = inspect both passages and their context; progress = identify which part of the question each passage addresses. This is an illustrative design, not a doctrinal conclusion.

Use the strongest truthful framing for the audience. When comparing variants, keep the promised content and evidential standard constant. A sober concern can open a study; move into explanation and learner capability as evidence becomes available. Do not repeatedly revive anxiety after the content has answered it.

Two honest packaging possibilities for the same context lesson are “How the surrounding verses clarify this promise” (toward understanding) and “Could this quotation lose its meaning outside its context?” (away from misinterpretation). The source must actually support the promised examination. Neither framing establishes that the audience belongs to a type; test relevance without changing the evidence or escalating the concern.

## Build interest through learning

Meaningful reasons, choice, manageable challenge, and respectful feedback are the foundation (M3–M4). Use these craft options according to the content:

1. **Make the gap specific.** Show enough known context that the learner can name the question. A small unresolved contrast or apparently incompatible detail is more usable than an opaque teaser.
2. **Invite a prediction when it helps.** Ask what the next piece of evidence might show, provide enough context to make an attempt, then reveal and explain it. Offer “I'm unsure” or direct access to the explanation. Predicting is a design option with experimental support in a particular learning task (C2), not a universal requirement.
3. **Deliver earned answers throughout.** Each substantial section resolves something and equips the next question. Maintain an opening question, its resolution location, and any necessary follow-on question in the outline. Avoid accumulating unresolved threads that require needless mental tracking.
4. **Vary what the learner does.** Move among noticing, comparing, tracing, explaining, recalling, and applying when those actions serve different jobs. An animation, story, diagram, or question should reveal something that ordinary prose alone leaves difficult to understand.
5. **Make progress concrete.** “You can now distinguish the quotation from its interpretation” describes a capability. Points, streaks, applause, or a progress bar cannot establish that capability.
6. **Keep discovery accessible.** Give definitions, a visible location in the argument, usable controls, and an optional deeper source trail. Challenge should come from the idea, while avoidable interface effort stays low.

This sequence is adaptable. Do not force every section into a repeated question/reveal/quiz card. Preserve storytelling, beauty, quiet reading, humor that fits the audience, and moments of reflection. The standard is whether they help this learner stay with this material and understand it.

### Curiosity and the dopamine question

Curiosity research connects anticipation and learning with activity in reward-related and memory-related brain regions (C1). Dopamine research also concerns learning from differences between expected and received reward (C3). These findings do not let a writer measure or prescribe an audience's dopamine response from a hook, graphic, cut, or page interaction.

Translate the evidence into an honest learning opportunity: pose an answerable question, make it relevant, let the learner inspect the evidence, deliver a worthwhile answer, and invite the next useful question. This is a **design inference** whose success must be evaluated behaviorally. Do not describe it as dopamine hacking, brain chemistry optimization, addiction, a guaranteed reward loop, or a mechanism that proves the teaching true.

Avoid variable-reward schedules, streak-loss pressure, forced continuation, concealed answers for watch time, engineered anxiety, and artificial scarcity. A learner should be able to stop with a complete answer and return voluntarily. Reward means the actual satisfaction and utility of understanding; it does not require a game or a chemical claim.

### Emotion with an evidence handoff

Let emotion express the true human meaning of a passage, question, or sourced story. Emotion can make some details more memorable while other context is missed (C4). After an emotional scene, give the learner the supporting passage, the specific relation it illustrates, and its limits. Check that the intended idea survives when the scene or soundtrack is removed. Do not use intensity as the measure of teaching quality.

For an actual difficult consequence or warning in Scripture, preserve the passage and context. Present the stakes proportionately, distinguish interpretation, acknowledge uncertainty where it exists, and allow the learner to examine the evidence. Do not suppress hard subjects or inflate them to secure attention.

## Carry interest through each format

| Moment | Website or study | YouTube content |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Recognizable question, relevant promise, immediate foothold | Confirm the package and begin useful content promptly |
| New evidence | Visible passage/context, useful comparison, local definition | Hold the relevant text or diagram while explaining that part |
| Uncertainty | Optional prediction, accessible answer, explanation | Spoken prediction/pause invitation, then a timely resolution |
| Progress | A meaningful distinction becomes clear; show the path | Brief synthesis or a purposeful change of representation |
| Return | Revisit a key idea with a new question or application | Recall an earlier distinction before applying it to new evidence |
| Exit | Complete answer, source trail, optional review or next question | Land the conclusion, offer a recall cue, and name a truthful continuation |

Judge engagement changes alongside comprehension and later memory. A learner finishing sooner because the answer is clear may be a successful outcome. An increase in watch time with worse understanding is a reason to revise the treatment. Use the evaluation plan in `memory-and-learning.md`.
