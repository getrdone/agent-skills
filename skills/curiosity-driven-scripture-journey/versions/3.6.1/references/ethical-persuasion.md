## Help someone judge the case

Persuasion in this skill means presenting truthful, relevant reasons clearly enough that someone can understand, examine, and voluntarily respond. State the position being advocated. Keep the learning available to someone who remains unconvinced. Scripture and approved sources govern the theological case; psychological findings supply no doctrinal authority.

In the existing concept/outline, record the proposed understanding or action, the evidence supporting it, a material objection or limit, and the learner's choice. A concern is addressed in proportion to its basis. Toward goals can include understanding, hope, competence, service, or another purpose actually relevant to the audience. Away concerns can include a real misunderstanding or consequence; never invent a fear to complete a matrix. The four motivation prompts do not classify people into fixed types.

## Build a case someone can inspect

For an important conclusion, make these relationships explicit in natural prose:

- **Claim:** the precise conclusion being proposed, with scope.
- **Evidence:** the passage, source, observation, or example and its context.
- **Reasoning:** why that evidence supports this conclusion; separate an observation from an interpretation.
- **Limit or fair challenge:** what the evidence does not settle, what another plausible reading notices, or what additional information would change the conclusion.
- **Choice:** the next useful question or optional action, without a demand for agreement.

This is an editorial reasoning aid, not a mandatory five-part paragraph. A simple point may need only a clear sentence and source. A disputed inference needs more explanation. Keep competing interpretations proportionate to their relevance and evidence; do not invent false balance or weaken an established fact just to simulate debate.

Research on involvement and persuasion illustrates that argument quality and source cues can play different roles under different conditions (P1). The design response here is to make relevance and evidence easier to consider. Do not exploit low attention, overwhelm a learner with pace, or substitute prestige for reasons.

## Use credibility accurately

Establish why a source is relevant to the particular claim. Verify credentials when they matter, provide the source trail, acknowledge uncertainty, and correct errors plainly. Confidence, production polish, a brain prop, audience size, testimonials, and popularity cannot establish truth or learning effectiveness.

Personal testimony can explain an experience; state whose experience it is and what can reasonably be inferred. It does not prove a general cause, outcome, or doctrine. Historical anecdotes and quotations require their own sourcing before reuse, even when told vividly by a respected presenter.

Nonverbal delivery can affect how a message is received. The popular 7/38/55 percentages concern a limited feelings/attitudes setting and do not allocate the importance of teaching content, logic, or persuasion (P3). Write the case well and deliver it understandably.

## Invite thought through narrative and emotion

A sourced story can make a question concrete, show a decision and its consequences, or make another person's perspective understandable. Identify why this case matters, then return to evidence and its limits. Narrative-persuasion research (P2) gives a reason to preserve that handoff: absorption in a story can affect belief, and labeling fiction alone did not eliminate the effect in the studied tasks. Do not aim to suppress counterargument or make an invented scene feel like proof.

An authentic feeling may communicate the significance of a true event. Give learners room for their own response. They may understand a solemn passage without becoming visibly upset or understand an argument while disagreeing. Avoid treating emotional intensity, agreement, applause, or a moving soundtrack as evidence of understanding.

Curiosity invites a question and delivers an answer. Offer the most useful answer when it becomes supportable, then deepen the explanation or open a genuinely new question. A narrative may reveal events in an intelligible order; it must not withhold core teaching solely for watch time. Avoid forced suspense, secrecy, outrage, or vague promises of a later revelation.

Negative wording can increase news clicks in a studied context (P12). That finding does not establish better learning, a universal dominant fear motive, or a reason to escalate religious anxiety. Evaluate the audience's real concern and the quality of understanding separately from attention metrics.

## Make invitations clear and voluntary

Name the action, its purpose, what it involves, and any relevant commitment or limitation. Offer a proportionate next step: inspect a passage, compare a source, try a reading practice, ask a question, save a review card, or continue to a related study. Let someone decline or leave with a complete answer.

Avoid manufactured scarcity, repeated pressure after a refusal, public pledges used to bind a learner, hidden escalation of commitment, shame, threats to belonging, or a moral/spiritual score for compliance. A freely chosen reflection is not an obligation to disclose private beliefs or experiences. The hard bans in `story-craft.md` continue to apply; “ethical persuasion” is not an exception to them.

Use consistent key wording as a cue to reasoning and sources. Do not repeat an unsupported claim until it feels familiar: experiments on illusory truth show why familiarity is an inadequate truth check (P11). In a misconception correction, identify the error only as needed, state the accurate replacement, and explain its basis. Return to the evidence through varied recall or application, not through a repetition quota.

## Review the intended effect

Ask whether a learner could restate the case, find its evidence, recognize its limit, and freely choose a response. Separate these outcomes:

| Outcome | Possible observation | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | Relevance feedback, continued viewing | Comprehension or agreement |
| Understanding | Explanation of the evidence and inference | Durable recall |
| Memory / transfer | Delayed explanation and a new application | Spiritual worth or commitment |
| Judgment | Calibrated confidence, fair account of an alternative | That the favored conclusion is automatically true |
| Voluntary action | A self-chosen next step | Inner belief, sincerity, or permanent behavior change |

For audience pilots, use the existing learning evaluation plan. Compare one meaningful feature at a time while keeping evidence and the promised outcome constant. Invite feedback on pressure, confusion, respect, and usefulness alongside comprehension. Do not collect religious profiles to optimize persuasion. A design review identifies plausible improvements; it cannot establish a measured learning or persuasion gain.
