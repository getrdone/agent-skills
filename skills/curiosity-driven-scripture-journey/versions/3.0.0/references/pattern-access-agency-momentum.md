# Access, Agency, and Momentum Patterns

Load this family when equivalent access, learner control, perceivable interaction state, calm progress, reflection, or next-choice architecture is central. Evidence provenance lives in `learning-pattern-sources.md`.

## LP-AA-01 — Equivalent Modality Choice

**Learning job:** let more learners access the same meaning through useful forms without fragmenting the truth.

**Use when:** text, diagram, audio, video, timeline, or interaction can clarify the same content for different needs, contexts, or preferences.

**Avoid when:** one option omits evidence, each branch teaches a different conclusion, or “choice” merely duplicates decoration.

**Learner flow:** encounter one clear default → see labeled equivalent options → choose or combine forms → return to the same evidence path and payoff.

**Scripture adaptation:** offer passage text, approved audio where rights permit, a labeled structure diagram, and a plain transcript. Every form carries the same references and interpretive boundaries.

**Agent output contract:** define the shared learning outcome, content parity rules, default form, optional forms, and how state persists. Audit each form for missing claims or citations.

**Access and age:** modality is a choice and accommodation, not a fixed “learning style.” Do not infer preference from age or disability. Keep all controls keyboard-accessible and labels explicit.

**Evidence anchors:** CK-12 concept pages offer readings, videos, interactives, real-world applications, study guides, and practice; Khan Academy Kids offers adaptive paths plus a library of activities, books, videos, and creative lessons.

## LP-AA-02 — Read, Listen, Follow Along

**Learning job:** support decoding, attention, language access, and independent pacing by synchronizing spoken and written content.

**Use when:** a passage, quotation, story, or explanation benefits from narration or text-to-speech.

**Avoid when:** audio rights are unclear, narration is inaccurate, or synchronized highlighting creates visual overload without an off switch.

**Learner flow:** choose read myself or listen → play/pause and control speed → follow synchronized text or transcript → answer a comprehension or observation prompt → continue.

**Scripture adaptation:** identify translation and audio provider. Keep the complete passage text and citation available; never substitute an unapproved paraphrase as narration.

**Agent output contract:** include transcript, speaker labels where needed, playback controls, visible focus, and a non-audio path. Record rights and attribution.

**Access and age:** captions and transcript are not optional extras. Do not autoplay. Support screen readers without overlapping audio. Word highlighting may help early readers but must not be the only locator.

**Evidence anchors:** Khan Academy Kids offers “Read to Me” and “Read By Myself,” narration, and word highlighting; CodeAI exposes Immersive Reader and text-to-speech in lesson instructions; Khan Academy provides captions and accessibility preferences.

## LP-AA-03 — Multisensory State Feedback

**Learning job:** make the current state and consequences of an interaction perceivable through more than one channel.

**Use when:** a learner toggles, drags, filters, sequences, plays a timeline, or changes a dynamic model.

**Avoid when:** sound or animation is ornamental, distracting, or the only signal.

**Learner flow:** action → immediate visible and textual state change → optional sound or voicing → persistent current-state summary → reversible next action.

**Scripture adaptation:** when a translation or context window changes, update the visible label, passage text, focus state, and screen-reader announcement. When chronology changes, state the selected event and relationship in text.

**Agent output contract:** define visual, text, focus, and optional auditory feedback for every state; include reset; test with keyboard and without color, sound, animation, or precise pointing.

**Access and age:** use polite live announcements for ordinary updates and reserve assertive alerts for genuine errors. Provide sound controls. Large targets and enhanced touch benefit many ages.

**Evidence anchors:** PhET pairs interactive descriptions, alternative input, highlights, pan/zoom, sound or sonification, and live state descriptions; its accessible simulations expose dynamic state through a parallel document structure.

## LP-AA-04 — Visible Progress, Tiny Win

**Learning job:** orient the learner and make accumulating understanding feel real.

**Use when:** the journey spans several chunks or sessions and the learner needs to know what has been answered and what remains.

**Avoid when:** progress is trivial, tied to pressure, or rewards clicks instead of learning.

**Learner flow:** see the route or current place → complete one meaningful unit → receive a concise payoff → update progress → see the next useful step.

**Scripture adaptation:** use calm labels such as “Question named,” “Context checked,” “Evidence compared,” and “Answer formed.” A tiny win is a real insight, not points or a badge.

**Agent output contract:** define progress units from learning milestones, not viewport scroll alone. Pair every progress change with a content payoff and allow resumption.

**Access and age:** expose progress in text and programmatic state. Do not use streak loss, countdowns, leaderboards, hearts, or fear of falling behind. Keep celebrations optional and brief.

**Evidence anchors:** Duolingo, Khan Academy, and Brilliant make route and progress visible; the project adapts this as orientation without manipulative gamification.

## LP-AA-05 — Reflect, Transfer, Choose Next Curiosity

**Learning job:** consolidate the promised payoff, connect it to another context, and preserve learner agency at the exit.

**Use when:** a segment or journey has delivered enough evidence for synthesis.

**Avoid when:** the answer has not yet been stated, reflection coerces personal disclosure, or the “next” choice is a disguised conversion pressure.

**Learner flow:** explicit answer → one-sentence synthesis prompt → optional transfer to another passage or practical question → featured next curiosity plus alternatives → learner chooses or exits.

**Scripture adaptation:** ask, “What part of the evidence changed or clarified the question?” Then offer the required featured direct continuation, two additional direct routes, one moderately related route, and one wildcard.

**Agent output contract:** state the answer before reflection. Provide a private or optional response mode, a transfer prompt, and labeled next paths whose relationship is honest.

**Access and age:** no response is required to continue or leave. Keep next links sequential and descriptive. For younger learners, allow drawing or oral reflection; for adults, avoid sentimental prompts that assume agreement.

**Evidence anchors:** Coursera and CodeAI use reflection, discussion, hands-on application, and capstone transfer; Khan Academy Kids allows guided or self-selected exploration; the project’s next-path architecture supplies the Scripture-specific branch set.
